schoolHow It's Calculated

expand_more

The Bite Score (0–100) blends six normalized ocean-condition layers using a fixed Weighted Linear Combination. Each factor is also available as its own toggleable layer above.

thermostat SST thermal-front score 33%

Sharpest sea-surface-temperature gradients (thermal fronts), where Yellowfin Tuna often forage along temperature breaks. Source: NASA MUR satellite SST (~1km).

grass Chlorophyll-front score 24%

Sharpest chlorophyll / water-colour gradients marking blue-water/green-water boundaries where baitfish concentrate. Source: NOAA VIIRS satellite chlorophyll-a (~4km).

waves Current-edge score 14%

Intensity of surface current-velocity edges / eddy boundaries. Source: Copernicus Marine surface current vectors (~9km).

water SSHA / eddy-front score 14%

Sharpest sea-surface-height-anomaly gradients, marking eddy edges via geostrophic shear -- often a cleaner eddy signal than current velocity alone. Source: Copernicus Marine SSHA (~9km).

layers MLD front score 6%

Shoaling (shallower) mixed layer depth compresses baitfish and forage species closer to the surface -- a signal that matters most where it coincides with the thermal or chlorophyll fronts already scored above. Source: Copernicus Marine mixed layer depth (mlotst, ~9km).

terrain Depth-suitability score 9%

Fuzzy suitability envelope peaking at the continental shelf-break / drop-off (favoured by pelagic gamefish). Land is masked out entirely. Source: composite bathymetry -- GEBCO (~450m) with real local multibeam/LiDAR surveys merged in nearshore, wherever available.

All layers are resampled onto the fine bathymetry grid (bilinear) before blending, so coarser satellite/model fields don't destroy the fine shelf-break detail.

layersMixed Layer Depth (MLD) Front

expand_more

A shoaling (shallower) mixed layer compresses baitfish and forage species closer to the surface, within easier reach of surface-feeding tuna -- particularly meaningful where it coincides with the thermal/chlorophyll fronts already scored above.

travel_explore Data source

Copernicus Marine mixed layer thickness (mlotst), the same physics product family already used for SSHA/currents above.

grid_on Method

Displayed here as the raw depth field (metres) on its own percentile-clipped scale, brighter where the mixed layer is shallower. Ripley is still wiring this into a gradient/front-strength score alongside the other four WLC factors above.

colorize Reading it

Brighter areas are a shallower (shoaled) mixed layer; darker areas are deeper mixing -- look for bright patches lining up with the SST/chlorophyll front layers above.

Toggle on “Factor: MLD front score” in the Layers list above. Not available for every date -- the Copernicus mixed layer depth fetch degrades gracefully (skipped, with a warning) if it fails for that date, the same as FSLE below.

Reading it: this layer shows the raw mlotst depth field (metres, see its own mini legend once toggled on) -- not the normalized 0-100 gradient/front-strength value that actually feeds the weight above, which isn’t currently exported as its own visual layer.

airOcean Fronts (FSLE)

expand_more

A Finite-Size Lyapunov Exponent (FSLE) highlights the thin, filamentary ocean fronts where converging surface currents concentrate drifting plankton and baitfish -- a Lagrangian signal invisible in any single day's current snapshot.

travel_explore Data source

Free daily surface current vectors (u/v) from the Copernicus Marine Data Store, including several forecast days beyond the selected date.

scatter_plot Method

A dense grid of virtual particle pairs is advected forward through the current field with a 4th-order Runge-Kutta integrator. Each pair's separation is tracked until it grows from an initial distance δ₀ to a final threshold δf, taking time τ.

functions Formula

λ = (1⁄τ) · ln(δf ⁄ δ₀), in units of 1/day. Brighter ridges mean faster separation, i.e. sharper convergence fronts.

Toggle on “Ocean fronts (FSLE)” in the Layers list above. Not available for every date -- it requires several days of forecast surface-current data beyond the selected date, which isn’t always available (e.g. for far-past historical dates).

waterSea Level Anomaly (Altimetry)

expand_more

Satellite radar altimetry measures the sea surface height relative to a long-term mean, revealing warm-core and cold-core eddies, EAC meanders, and upwelling zones -- features that concentrate baitfish and pelagic species.

satellite_alt Data source

NOAA CoastWatch ERDDAP — nesdisSSH1day: multi-satellite merged altimetry product from the RADS database (Jason-3, Sentinel-3A, CryoSat-2, SARAL/AltiKa). 0.25° daily global grid, archive from 2017 onward. Approx. 3-day near-real-time latency.

palette Colour scale

Red = positive SLA (sea surface elevated above mean — warm-core EAC eddy or southward EAC jet). Blue = negative SLA (sea surface below mean — cold-core eddy or coastal upwelling). Scale clipped at ±0.4 m to suit typical EAC anomaly amplitudes.

info How to use

Strong positive SLA cells (red) indicate warm-core eddies that spin off the EAC and can concentrate juvenile tuna and baitfish at their edges. The sharpest edges and saddle-points between eddies of opposite sign are often the most productive fishing zones. Use this layer alongside the FSLE overlay to confirm which fronts are associated with real eddy structure.

Toggle on “Sea level anomaly (altimetry)” in the Layers list above. Not available for every date — archive begins 2017-02-13, and there may be gaps during satellite outages or processing delays.

sciencev2 Bite Score (Beta)BETA

expand_more

This is a separate, experimental scoring model -- not a refinement of the Bite Score above. It combines seafloor-structure proximity (shelf break + named canyons), real vorticity-based warm-core eddy detection, a bell-curve SST fit, an optimal-band chlorophyll fit, and a seasonal multiplier, using different weights and different inputs than v1. It has not been validated against catch data the way v1's model has -- treat it as a second opinion to compare against v1, not a replacement for it.

thermostat SST bell-curve score 33%

How close sea-surface temperature (blended with its local gradient) sits to Yellowfin Tuna's preferred range, scored as a bell curve rather than v1's pure thermal-front-gradient approach -- peaks away from fronts too, not just on them.

grass Chlorophyll optimal-band score 24%

Chlorophyll concentration scored against an optimal productivity band (too little = clear/oligotrophic water, too much = turbid/coastal water), rather than v1's pure chlorophyll-gradient/front approach.

waves Current-edge score (v2 recomputed) 14%

Surface current-velocity edge intensity, recomputed on v2's wider AOI_V2 grid alongside the other v2 factors.

terrain Depth-suitability score (v2 recomputed) 9%

Same depth-suitability envelope concept as v1, recomputed on v2's own wider-coverage bathymetry grid (data/gebco_seq_v2.nc).

layers MLD gradient score (v2) 6%

Mixed-layer-depth gradient, normalized into a real 0-100 WLC factor (unlike v1's raw-metres MLD diagnostic layer).

hub Structure proximity score 8%

Proximity to named seafloor structure (shelf break, canyons) -- a genuinely NEW v2 factor with no v1 equivalent. See config.STRUCTURE_FEATURES_V2 / structure_layers.py.

cyclone Eddy detection score 6%

Real vorticity-based warm-core eddy detection (from surface current shear), rather than v1's SSHA-gradient proxy -- another genuinely NEW v2 factor with no v1 equivalent.

calendar_month Seasonal multiplier

The combined score above is further scaled by a calendar-month multiplier: ×1.0 Sep–Nov (peak season), ×0.8 Dec–Jan and May–Aug (winter “barrel run”), ×0.3 Feb–Apr (seasonal low).

Only available for a small number of test dates so far -- if it hasn’t been run for the date you’re viewing, the status above will say so and the heatmap toggle simply stays blank (v1’s heatmap is completely unaffected either way).

expand_more

This is a second, independent, experimental scoring model -- built from a real SEQ fisherman podcast account's informally-described conditions, not a refinement of v1 or v2 above. It combines EAC axis-position and convergence-point scores, a distance-offshore ramp, an upwelling/downwelling vorticity score, its own SST bell curve and depth-suitability ramp, using different weights and different inputs than either v1 or v2. It has not been validated against catch data -- treat it as an informal, experimental third opinion, not a replacement for v1 or v2.

toggle_on Viewing it

Toggle on “Bite Score heatmap Lenigas (SEQ fisherman model)” in the Layers list above (alongside the v1, legacy and v2 heatmaps) to compare it directly against them. Its 6 individual WLC factors plus an informational wind-speed layer are also available lower down in the Layers list, each labelled “Factor (Lenigas): ...”.

alt_route EAC axis-position score 21%

5-zone score based on signed distance from the East Australian Current's traced core-jet axis -- the podcast's account of fishing relative to 'the current line' turned into a continuous km-based score.

join_inner EAC convergence-point score 20%

Proximity to a single detected point where westward-flowing water most strongly meets the EAC's core-jet axis -- an honestly-documented v1-of-Lenigas simplification (falls back to a flat neutral 50 on days with no detected convergence).

terrain Depth-suitability score (Lenigas) 18%

A separate depth-suitability ramp/envelope tuned to this model's own preferred depth band, recomputed on the same AOI_V2 bathymetry grid as v2's depth-suitability factor.

swap_vert Upwelling/downwelling score 16%

Continuous tanh-scaled score from relative vorticity sign -- upwelling (nutrient-rich water rising) scores higher than downwelling, per the podcast's account of upwelling zones fishing better.

thermostat SST bell-curve score (Lenigas) 14%

A clean Gaussian bell-curve fit around this model's own preferred sea-surface temperature, without v2's added gradient/front blending -- SST is this model's lowest-weighted factor, per the source account's own de-emphasis of exact temperature.

waves SSHA hotspot score (Lenigas) 10%

Percentile-tiered score from a per-date, re-based sea surface height anomaly (zos) -- a hotspot/boundary signal distinct from upwelling/downwelling's current-shear-derived vorticity, using seasonal anchor tables that peak at the 30th percentile (upwelling-biased).

air Wind speed

Real wind speed, fetched and reported for validation purposes but NOT yet part of the WLC weights below -- shown here only as an informational raw layer (own percentile-clipped scale, not a 0-100 score) until a wind-suitability curve is specified as follow-up work.

Only available for a small number of test dates so far -- if it hasn’t been run for the date you’re viewing, the status above will say so and the heatmap toggle simply stays blank (v1's and v2's heatmaps are completely unaffected either way).

wavesGT Bite ScoreEXPERIMENTAL

expand_more

This is a third, independent, experimental scoring model — targeting Giant Trevally and other large reef ambush predators, not Yellowfin Tuna. GT are inshore reef specialists: they sit on the western (inshore) pressure edge of the EAC, not the eastern slack zone, and ambush prey over 20–150 m reef structure. This model is species-specific and uses completely different weights and depth thresholds than v1, v2 or Lenigas. It has not been validated against catch data — treat it as an informal, experimental species layer.

Checking GT availability...

toggle_on Viewing it

Toggle on “Bite Score heatmap GT (Giant Trevally reef model)” in the Layers list above. Its 6 individual contributing factors are also available, each labelled “Factor (GT): …”.

alt_route EAC pressure-edge score 20%

Score based on signed distance from the EAC core-jet axis. GT sit on the WESTERN (inshore) side of the EAC where the southward current presses against reef structure -- the highest scores are 5-15 km inshore of the axis, opposite to the YFT model's eastern-slack-zone peak.

swap_vert Upwelling score 18%

Continuous tanh-scaled score from relative vorticity sign: SH-clockwise (upwelling) scores 100, SH-anticyclonic (downwelling) scores 0. Upwelling drives baitfish concentration at reef edges where GT ambush.

terrain Depth suitability (GT) 15%

Trapezoidal depth envelope for GT reef habitat: ramps up from 5 m, prime zone 15–30 m (shallow reef crests, bommies and hard structure), declines to zero at 60 m. GT ambush from shallow reef structure, not deep-water.

landscape Depth structure (reef edges) 17%

Bathymetric gradient: how steeply the seafloor changes depth. GT are ambush predators that sit on hard structural edges — reef ledges, bommies, shelf steps — and wait for baitfish swept past by the EAC. Score = 100 × tanh(|∇depth| / 5 m·km⁻¹). High values = sharp depth edges = prime GT ambush habitat.

thermostat SST suitability (GT) 12%

Gaussian bell-curve SST score peaking at 26°C (the warm end of EAC surface water in SE QLD). GT prefer warm EAC water; higher temperature indicates a stronger EAC incursion pushing baitfish into reef pressure edges.

dark_mode Moon phase score 9%

GT are low-light ambush predators: darkness is their advantage. New moon (days 1–2) scores 100; full moon (day 14) scores 20. Spatially uniform — same value everywhere for a given date. Included as a WLC factor so it appears as its own map layer.

speed Current speed gradient 5%

Spatial gradient of current speed magnitude: high values where the current changes speed rapidly (e.g. EAC edge meeting slower inshore water). GT ambush from these speed change boundaries, so high gradient = good feeding edge.

air North wind score 4%

Northerly wind preference: max(0, cos(wind_dir))¹⋅⁵ scaled to 0–100. North wind scores 100; south wind scores 0. North winds concentrate baitfish against SE QLD reef faces. Data from ASCAT 7-day composite (~5–6 week lag; weight redistributed if unavailable).

calendar_month Seasonal multiplier

Combined score is scaled by a seasonal multiplier (×1.0 Sep–Nov, ×0.9 Jan/Dec, ×0.8 Jun–Aug, ×0.6 Feb–Apr). Moon phase is already baked into the WLC as a factor layer (moon phase score above).

Only available for dates where the GT pipeline has been run — if it hasn’t, the status above will say so and the heatmap toggle stays blank. v1, v2 and Lenigas heatmaps are completely unaffected either way.

hubOuterline Bite ScoreEXPERIMENTAL

expand_more

This is a fourth, independent, experimental scoring model for Yellowfin Tuna, built on a different principle from v1/v2/Lenigas/GT above: it rewards CO-LOCATION of multiple favourable oceanographic features (current convergence, SST/FSLE/SLA fronts, the EAC's outer boundary, a bait-concentration proxy...) rather than any single-factor extreme, and treats depth as a moderate-weight FILTER only -- never a dominant factor. A non-linear multiplier (up to 1.25×) rewards pixels where 6 or more of these features co-occur. It was validated against one real historical event (11 Oct 2023, Point Lookout) without tuning to fit it, but has not been validated against a broader catch-data record -- treat it as an informal, experimental fifth opinion.

Checking Outerline availability...

toggle_on Viewing it

Toggle on “Bite Score heatmap Outerline (Yellowfin outer-line convergence model)” in the Layers list above. Its 11 individual contributing factors plus an informational feature-convergence-count layer are also available, each labelled “Factor (Outerline): …”.

join_inner Current convergence score 15%

Highest where surface currents converge (negative horizontal divergence) -- convergence zones concentrate drifting plankton and baitfish. The single highest-weighted Outerline factor.

timeline FSLE front score (Outerline) 12%

Finite-Size Lyapunov Exponent front strength, normalized to 0-100 -- unlike v1 (where FSLE is an independent diagnostic layer only), Outerline includes it as a genuine WLC factor. Gracefully degrades (weight redistributed) when forecast current data isn't available.

device_thermostat SST front score (Outerline) 12%

Sharpest sea-surface-temperature gradients (thermal fronts), recomputed on the Outerline model's own AOI grid -- the same underlying signal as v1's SST factor, reused here as one of several co-located boundary features.

alt_route EAC outer-boundary score 10%

A Gaussian bump peaking ~12 km east of the traced EAC core-jet axis -- rewards the current's OUTER boundary/edge itself, not the core and not open water far from any EAC influence.

speed Current shear/interaction score 10%

Normalized gradient magnitude of current speed -- shear zones/boundaries where fast core water meets slower surrounding water, distinct from the convergence factor's divergence-sign signal.

grass Bait concentration proxy 10%

A composite of a band-pass chlorophyll suitability score (moderate, 'green-on-blue-edge' concentration -- neither bloom nor gin-clear water) and the mean of the SST front, current convergence and FSLE front scores -- baitfish concentrate where productive water coincides with physical aggregating structure, not from chlorophyll alone.

thermostat SST suitability score (Outerline) 8%

A broad, forgiving piecewise-linear suitability curve peaking in the 23-26°C range -- deliberately gentler than v1/Lenigas/GT's SST curves since suitability alone is low-weighted here; the sharper SST front factor above carries the boundary signal.

water SLA / eddy-boundary score 8%

Rewards the GRADIENT of sea-surface-height anomaly (eddy/current edges), not the absolute SLA value -- so it marks eddy boundaries rather than just re-scoring the eddy core.

swap_vert Upwelling/downwelling transition score 7%

Peaks at the zero-crossing between upwelling and downwelling (the TRANSITION zone), decaying toward zero deep inside either regime -- the opposite shape from Lenigas's score, which rewards one vorticity sign outright.

terrain Depth-suitability score (Outerline) 5%

A wide, flat 'good' plateau from ~200 m to 4000 m+ -- deliberately NOT a narrow ideal band like v1/Lenigas/GT's depth curves, per real catch evidence (11 Oct 2023, ~250 m) that yellowfin hold well short of deep water. Only 5% weight -- a moderate FILTER, never a dominant factor.

air Wind & visibility score 1%

A 50/50 blend of a sighting/spotting-conditions score (best in a flat calm, decaying with wind speed) and a neutral 0.5 ocean-mechanism placeholder. Lowest-weighted (1%) Outerline factor; gracefully degrades when ASCAT wind data isn't available.

join_full Feature convergence count info

How many of 8 tracked factors (SST front, EAC boundary, current convergence/interaction, FSLE front, SLA boundary, upwelling/downwelling, bait proxy) independently score ≥65/100 at that pixel -- drives a non-linear multiplier (1.00× up to 1.25× at 6+ features) rewarding genuine co-location of favourable conditions. Informational only, not itself part of the WLC weights.

Only available for dates where the Outerline pipeline has been run — if it hasn’t, the status above will say so and the heatmap toggle stays blank. v1, v2, Lenigas and GT heatmaps are completely unaffected either way.

terrainBathymetry Relief Map

expand_more

A single shaded-relief (hillshade) view of the sea floor across the whole map area -- one toggle, but really 4 image layers stacked together so every real survey shows at its own best resolution, not averaged down onto one common grid.

public Data source

Geoscience Australia’s AusBathyTopo 2024 grid (250m, whole-of-AOI) as the base, with the AusSeabed Moreton Bay Approaches (30m) and Mudjimba Island (0.5m) multibeam surveys and the Sunshine Coast LiDAR survey (10m) layered on top wherever each one covers the AOI -- GEBCO (~450m) is used only as a last-resort fallback outside all of those.

grid_on Method

Each source is kept at ITS OWN native pixel resolution rather than being resampled onto one shared grid -- so the small local surveys never lose real detail, and the whole-AOI base isn’t artificially smoothed by being upsampled onto a finer grid than it actually has data for. Rendered with fixed real-metre depth/elevation colour bands (finer near the surface) rather than a smooth gradient, so depth changes stay visible even across a range spanning land down to the deep continental slope.

colorize Reading it

Blue shades are underwater depth (darker = deeper); green/brown shades are land. Look for visibly sharper, crisper detail over the Sunshine Coast, Moreton Bay approaches and Mudjimba Island -- the areas backed by the finest real survey data, now shown at their true native resolution.

Toggle on “Bathymetry” in the bottom-left map control (next to the basemap switcher). Loaded on demand from the local dashboard server (python -m bite_score.webapp) the first time it’s switched on.

mapMBGFC Fishing Chart

expand_more

A scanned nautical chart published by the Moreton Bay Game Fish Club, showing named reefs, banks and fishing grounds from Noosa to the Gold Coast, georeferenced onto the map using its own printed lat/lon grid.

description Data source

MBGFC "Fishing Locations & Info" one-pager PDF (mbgfc.com.au) -- a scanned chart image plus a text table of exact FAD/SFAD/wave-buoy coordinates and depths.

grid_on Method

The chart's own printed degree gridlines (153–155°E, 26–28°S) were located pixel-by-pixel and used to fit a linear lon/lat transform; the FAD/SFAD/wave-buoy markers are parsed directly from the PDF's text table, not estimated from the image.

warning Caveat

Reference only, not for navigation -- print/scan registration may introduce small (sub-100m scale) positional error in the chart image itself.

Toggle on “Fishing chart”, “FADs/buoys” and “Waypoints” using the buttons in the bottom-left map control. Loaded on demand from the local dashboard server (python -m bite_score.webapp) the first time each is switched on.
